About K12Info

K12Info is a school exploration tool designed to make publicly available information about U.S. K–12 schools easier to search, compare, and understand.

The information presented by K12Info is derived from publicly available datasets published by the United States government. K12Info brings information from multiple government datasets together and presents it through a searchable map and individual school pages, making school directory, enrollment, staffing, program, and discipline information easier to explore in one place. Both public and private schools are included where data is available.

Data Sources

For public schools, K12Info uses publicly available government data from sources including the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES) Common Core of Data (CCD) and the U.S. Department of Education Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC). Private school information is based on publicly available NCES private school datasets.

These datasets are collected independently and may represent different reporting years. K12Info uses school identifiers and data crosswalks to associate records between datasets where necessary.

Data Accuracy and Limitations

Some information displayed on K12Info may appear inconsistent with other information about the same school. For example, enrollment totals, grade levels, program participation, or other statistics may not always align perfectly.

There are several reasons this can occur. Source datasets may cover different reporting years, schools may change names, grade configurations, enrollment, or organizational structure between reporting periods, and records from separate datasets may need to be associated through identifier crosswalks. These crosswalks do not always produce a perfect one-to-one relationship between records.

The original government datasets may also contain missing, suppressed, unavailable, or incorrectly reported values. K12Info generally presents information based on the records supplied by these sources rather than attempting to independently determine what a school's correct value should have been.

As a result, information on K12Info should be treated as an informational reference rather than an authoritative source. Users making important educational or enrollment decisions should verify relevant information directly with the school, school district, or appropriate government source.
